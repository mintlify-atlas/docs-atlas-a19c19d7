This notebook presents an investment scenario built around Inditex (ticker: ITX) — the parent company of Zara — using historical daily stock prices to simulate a systematic investment strategy. A hypothetical investor receives their salary on the last Thursday of each month and immediately invests **€50** in ITX shares on the last Friday of that month (the following day), purchasing at the opening price (or the next available trading day if that Friday is a market holiday). All shares are sold on 28 December 2017 at the closing price, with a 2 % broker fee applied to each purchase. The notebook calculates the final portfolio value and total return, while also performing exploratory time-series analysis of the full price history.

## Dataset

The source file is `stocks-ITX.csv` (semicolon-delimited), containing **4,218 rows** — one per trading day — and **3 columns**.

| Column | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `Fecha` | object → datetime64 | Trading date in Spanish abbreviated format (e.g. `"23-may-2001"`) |
| `Cierre` | float64 | Closing price in euros |
| `Apertura` | float64 | Opening price in euros |

The raw file is sorted in reverse chronological order (newest first). The dataset spans from **23 May 2001** (ITX IPO date) through **28 December 2017**, covering approximately 16.6 years of trading.

## Analysis

### Data Loading and Cleaning

```python
import pandas as pd
import datetime as dt
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
plt.style.use('bmh')
import numpy as np

# Allow full display of all rows during exploration
pd.set_option('display.max_rows', None)

# Load the CSV (semicolon-delimited)
data = pd.read_csv('stocks-ITX.csv', sep=';')

# Reverse the chronological order so the earliest date is first
data = data.iloc[::-1, :].reset_index(drop=True)
```

### Date Parsing

The `Fecha` column uses Spanish abbreviated month names (`ene`, `feb`, `mar`, etc.), which are not directly parseable by pandas. The notebook replaces each abbreviated Spanish month name with its two-digit numeric equivalent using `str.replace`, then converts the column to `datetime64`:

```python
meses_en_esp = ['ene', 'feb', 'mar', 'abr', 'may', 'jun',
                'jul', 'ago', 'set', 'oct', 'nov', 'dic']
for index, mes in enumerate(meses_en_esp):
    data['Fecha'] = data['Fecha'].str.replace(mes, str(index + 1).zfill(2), regex=False)

data['Fecha'] = pd.to_datetime(data['Fecha'])
```

### Feature Engineering

Three new columns are derived from the parsed date:

```python
data['Dia_semana'] = data['Fecha'].dt.day_name()   # e.g. "Wednesday"
data['Mes']        = data['Fecha'].dt.month         # integer 1–12
data['Año']        = data['Fecha'].dt.year          # integer 2001–2017
```

### Trading Day Distribution

A horizontal bar chart of `Dia_semana.value_counts()` confirms that the dataset contains no weekend records, with trading days distributed across all five weekdays. September has the highest number of trading-day records (490), while February and December have the fewest (313 each).

### Price Time-Series Visualisation

A full opening-price time-series chart is produced to visualise the growth of ITX from its IPO price through its peak and subsequent movements up to late 2017. A supplementary `Valor_accion` column is added, classifying each day as `'revalorizó'` (day closed higher than open) or `'devaluó'` (day closed lower than or equal to open), using a custom `apply` function.

### Investment Simulation

The core calculation logic:

1. **Build a full calendar** — a complete daily calendar is created from the IPO date (23 May 2001) to 28 December 2017, with `Dia_semana`, `Mes`, and `Año` columns.
2. **Identify purchase days** — for each month, the last Friday (`Dia_semana == 'Friday'`) is identified. This is the day after the investor's last-Thursday payday.
3. **Find the next trading day** — if the last Friday falls on a market holiday (not present in the dataset), the next available trading date is selected by incrementing one day at a time until a match is found in the stock data.
4. **Purchase shares** — €50 is invested at the opening price of the selected day. The broker retains 2 % of each purchase, so the maximum whole number of shares buyable with €50 at the given price (including the 2 % fee) is computed: `n_acciones` is the largest integer `n` such that `n × Apertura × 1.02 ≤ 50`.
5. **Accumulate shares** — a running cumulative sum (`N_acciones_acum`) tracks the total shares held over time.
6. **Sell all shares** — the total accumulated shares are sold at the closing price on 28 December 2017 (`Cierre = 29.17`), yielding a final portfolio value of **€34,175.71**.
7. **Supplementary charts** — the number of shares purchased each month and the cumulative share count are plotted over time to illustrate the dollar-cost-averaging effect.

## Key Visualisations

- **Full daily close-price series** — illustrates the extraordinary long-term growth of ITX from IPO (May 2001) to December 2017.
- **Trading day frequency bar chart** — confirms clean weekday-only data with no weekend artefacts.
- **Monthly investment timeline** — marks each purchase date on the price chart, showing the dollar-cost-averaging effect over the 16-year period.

## Findings

- **ITX went public at ~€3.60** on 23 May 2001 and reached ~€29.17 at the close on 28 December 2017, representing approximately **8× price appreciation** over 16.6 years.
- **Final portfolio value: €34,175.71** — the result of investing €50 per month on the last Friday of each month from May 2001 through December 2017, after applying the 2 % broker fee at each purchase and selling all accumulated shares at the 28 December 2017 closing price.
- **All five weekdays appear** in the trading record; weekend entries are absent, confirming clean data.
- **September is the most data-rich month** (490 records), while February and December have the fewest (313 each), reflecting differences in market holiday frequency.
- The 2 % broker fee applied at each monthly purchase meaningfully reduces total return compared to a zero-fee scenario — a key parameter sensitivity highlighted by the notebook's simulation design.
- **Dollar-cost averaging** over the full period smooths out the impact of the significant price volatility visible in the mid-2000s and post-2008 periods.
